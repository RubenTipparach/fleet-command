From the culture, not from aircraft

Version 1 was fighter jets: wings and tail fins, things that fly in air. Nothing here flies. Each line of the Krai as we wrote them becomes part of the ship:

  • War is the only honest test: they meet the enemy face to face, so every hull is thin head-on and armoured edge first, and every direction is a weapon you charge with (a blade, a thrown lance, a dagger).
  • Forged steel, edged everything: the hull is one forged blade in volcanic gunmetal, honed bright at every edge, with no curves.
  • Knife-blade hulls, swept triangles: the blade is the hull itself, and the triangles are vanes, warheads and gun houses.
  • The forge: one drive like a furnace mouth, window slits and fissures lit forge orange.
  • A bird's head and wings (survey A8): a hooked beak for a ram, and vanes folded along the hull like a raptor's wings in a stoop, not spread to fly.
  • Rank is won in blood: a class grows by adding blades (the line, direction A). What the Krai call their ships, and whether a ship wears its kills, is yours to decide.

Three directions

  • A, Standing blade: a sword stood on its edge, diamond in section; beak ram forward, crest along its back, keel edge below.
  • B, Lance: a triangular warhead on a shaft, the crew block behind it, three fletching vanes.
  • C, Three-edged dagger: three blades round one core; the triangle in section, no up or down.

Windows give the scale

The same person-sized window as the Concord's, one row a deck 3.4 m apart, cut as narrow forge-lit slits on the hull's flats.

Not decided

Names, ranks and any marks of kills are the Krai's culture: questions in the survey (A15 to A17). No damage or hull lamps yet: those come once a direction is chosen.
