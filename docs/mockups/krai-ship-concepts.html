Redone on the guide

Versions 1 and 2 were drawn hull first and painted in the Krai console's own volcanic metal, red and forge orange. Version 4 follows the Ship Design Bible step by step; use Build it up to walk through it.

  • Rooms: 24 functional rooms on four decks, the middle two wide and the top and keel decks narrow, so the hull fitted round them is a blade's lozenge in section; the spinal cannon runs forward along the keel deck, so the bow is a wedge led from below.
  • Hull: fitted round them with the shared fitter, its chamfers cut as deep as the rooms allow, so the section is a blade standing on its edge.
  • Parts: an alien bayonet at the prow (polished mirror steel with lengthwise polishing streaks and a lit fuller, centred on the nose; on the frigate a thin needle), a dorsal crest and a keel blade (the Krai's distinct features), two blade nacelles standing on edge on swept struts, one tall forge drive. A frigate's bridge stays inside the hull (survey A18: a bridge block only on super-capitals).
  • Windows: person-sized, one row a deck, only where crew and command rooms meet the hull.
  • Exterior: blued steel in square-cut armour plates (chamfered corners, conduits, vent banks, hatches) and tempered bronze edges, pale gold light: the forge said a second way, chosen to complement the console (rule X1, a guide; nothing measures it).
  • Standard (the Concord line's, with the same shared code, lib/ship-skin.js): a detail tile of the Krai's own (hard-surface armour plates split and split again, square to the hull with 45 degree chamfered corners, recessed conduits with a pipe in them, vent slot banks, round hatches, bolt-hole rows, wear) as a normal map and a grime map, the seams tinted bronze; windows with a room behind each, drawn by interior mapping; six-sided thruster bells with a fairing and bolts, white-hot at the throat to forge orange at the lip; warm orange floodlights grazing the plating, with shadows; position lights, amber strobes and red beacons.
  • Rooms (provisional, the Krai's culture is yours to set): bare blued-steel walls ribbed like a rib cage, a steel grating floor, no carpet and no plants; forge-orange strip lamps and braziers, or dark red when the watch sleeps; a barracks of racked bunks, an armoury wall of blades, a mess round a brazier, and a command room with a war table lit from inside.

The classes

  • Four classes, each fitted round its own rooms by the same builder: corvette (47 m, three decks, twin bow guns, crew 14), frigate (87 m, four decks, a spinal cannon, crew 36), cruiser (126 m, five decks, two spinal cannons, one nacelle pair, crew 120) and dreadnought (211 m, seven decks, three spinal cannons, broadside batteries, crew 420).
  • The bayonet is a different shape on each class, more decoration than use: a barbed knife on the corvette, a thin needle on the frigate, a wide crescent standing across the nose on the dreadnought (smaller and more pointed on the cruiser), its horns raked back above and below the hull, a clean edge with two openings (owner: "I want a wide blade instead of long blade").
  • Livery, painted into the hull (owner: "ships dont have much liveries on main hull"): a crimson war stripe low on each flank that widens forward and sweeps up toward the bow, bronze pin lines either side, battle tallies in fives aft above it (3 on the corvette, 7 on the frigate, 12 on the cruiser, 19 on the dreadnought); nothing on the bow's top (owner: "I'd remove the triangle on top if the noses").
  • Nacelles: two glowing red panels stacked on the outboard face, each in a dark frame with a soft glow (owner: "they should look like glowing panels"), bronze collars at the ends, a raked fin above and a smaller one below, two clamped conduits inboard, and a glowing collector at the bow (owner: "nacelles still kinda overly simplistic").
  • Windows lie flat on one face of the section, never across a bend or a taper, and the room behind a window on a sloped face stays level (owner: "window geometry extending a bit over the hull slope", "window paralax on slope has the wrong room angle").
  • Only the dreadnought raises a command block above its spine (survey A18: a bridge block on super capital ships only); the others keep the bridge inside.
  • The frigate's plan was laid by hand; the others come from tools/mockups/krai_plans.py, a list of rooms per deck for each class, so a change to a class is a change to its list.

Damage

  • On the same shared code as the Concord line (lib/ship-fx.js): the same particles, colours with one meaning (orange fire, cyan-white coolant, violet plasma, sparks for power), weapon light, flashes, debris, beams and torpedoes.
  • A phaser leaves a scorch mark that glows hot and cools; a torpedo cuts a hole that shows the decks behind it, lit by what the plan puts there.
  • Nacelles vent plasma, spark, then the pair on that side breaks off and drifts away burning; the reactor leaks coolant from the belly under its room, jets it, the lamps stutter, then the core breaches; the bridge smokes, burns, then blows out; the guns spark, then burn at the muzzles; the drive's bells cool and stutter; life support goes dark a section at a time, windows and all.
  • The hull breaks apart as the Concord line's does, on the same shared code (lib/ship-break.js): it is cut into seeded cells when it is built (7 on the corvette to 14 on the dreadnought); a heavy hit on a badly damaged hull knocks off the cell it lands on; a core breach blows the outer cells off one by one, then the nacelles, the crest and the bayonet, then the main blast. Every piece burns and tumbles, its broken face shows the decks and frames it broke through, and cables stretch between pieces and snap.
