The Tern family

Every hull is lofted through octagonal stations, the five numbers per station of Star Crew's ship data, every corner chamfered. White armour over a grey subhull, a grey service band at the waterline with its pipe and clamps, grey channels down the back, a livery stripe that wraps the hull with a dark seam either side, the registry painted on the dorsal plating. Long octagonal nacelles with a cyan field strip each side and a copper intake. The Tern itself is drawn from its own data at Star Crew revision 95a49b2 as the yardstick.

The Concord's own

  • Concord blue stripes; a raised, glazed command block on every class.
  • The Swift and the Petrel each three ways: one nacelle below, one above and one below, and the family's level pair.
  • The Albatross in two hulls: a secondary hull under the primary on a neck, its pylons sweeping out of it.

Windows give the scale

Every window is a 1.0 by 0.62 m pane in a 0.12 m copper rim, one row per deck, 3.5 m apart, in runs of four with a frame between. Behind each is a room with depth (interior mapping): lit warm, cold, or dark. A section that loses life support goes dark window by window.

Detail and normal maps

One detail tile, 8 m along by 7.8 m round, in the paint's own plate grid: plate seams, inset and raised panels, rivet rows, hatches and vent grilles in a normal map, and grime, streaks and scratches in a wear map, projected from three sides so every face gets them.

Lit for the dark

Floodlight pools on the registry, the command block, each nacelle and along the waist graze the plating; windows glow; port red, starboard green, white strobes on the nacelles, red beacons. Try Shadow side and No sun.
