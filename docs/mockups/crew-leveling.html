    How it works

    Version 1 (you: "ensigns provide no bonus but gain xp overtime, and can be traded for prestige so you can get more experienced crew, or just level up your crew member", "need crew skill tree", "skill tree, each level gets a skill point, the player can auto level officers or pick paths on the tree to go down and specialize").

    Version 5 (you: "taller where the nodes branch out please, lets do branches, implement that", "implement the tree in the game"). Branches is the tree, taller where the skills fan out: more room between the lanes' second step and the crossovers, and between the crossovers and the merges below them. Lanes is gone. The game's crew screen draws the same tree.

    Version 4 (you: "branches are good, lanes are good, branches should be vertiacally inverted, starting from the top with the 3, squish them so theres not so much space between columns"). Two styles stay: Branches now grows down from its three roots at the top to the capstone at the foot, its lanes closer together, and Lanes runs left to right as before. Constellation and Radial are gone.

    Version 3 (you: "it doesnt have to be like an actual pyramid... just a skill tree. mockup a few different versions and make it look nice. use art drawing skills to make skill icons, and show tooltips on hover overs"). Each station has a skill tree of fourteen skills in three lanes, its three themes (for the helm: piloting, emergency turns and evasion). Version 3 drew the same tree four ways: Branches growing up from the station, Constellation, Radial, and Lanes.

    Each lane starts at a root. A crossover sits between two lanes and opens from either of them (a dashed line), so a path can change lanes. The keystone at the centre needs both crossovers, each lane's tip needs its own lane and the crossover beside it (solid lines: needs every one), and the capstone sits on the keystone. Every skill has a picture of what it changes, drawn to the icon standard; hover over one for what it does and what it needs, and click or tap it to learn it.

    Officers go from level 1 to level 10, with one skill point for every level after the first, so a level 1 ensign brings nothing to a battle. Ranks come with levels: Ensign at 1, Lieutenant at 2, Lt. Commander at 4, Commander at 6, Captain at 8. The cheapest way to the capstone is six points; a level 10 officer has nine, out of fourteen skills, so every officer has chosen.

    Every officer gains experience from each battle (more for a better result) and a little each turn aboard. Promote an officer once they have the experience for the next level; short of it, the rest can be paid in prestige. Transfer an officer out and the side gains their worth plus their banked experience in prestige, which hires an officer of a higher level from the pool. With Auto level on, points go up the centre to the capstone, then along the left lane. Retrain takes every point back for a little prestige.

    Version 2 drew each station's skills as a pyramid; version 1 as three separate branches.

    After the Starfleet Command 3 manual's officers (printed page 36: officers gain experience over a campaign, their worth and skills rising, and are swapped for more experienced ones for prestige; three skills to a station), in our own words and numbers.
