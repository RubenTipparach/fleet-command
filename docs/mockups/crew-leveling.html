    How it works

    Version 1 (you: "ensigns provide no bonus but gain xp overtime, and can be traded for prestige so you can get more experienced crew, or just level up your crew member", "need crew skill tree", "skill tree, each level gets a skill point, the player can auto level officers or pick paths on the tree to go down and specialize").

    Version 2 (you: "I wish crew leveling was kind of like a pyramid tree or something where some nodes can cross over, and some nodes have 2 or more prereqs"). Each station's skills are a pyramid: five at the base, then four, three, two and the apex. Most skills open from either of the two below them (a dashed line), so a path can cross from one side of the pyramid to the other. The centre skill of the middle tier and the apex need both skills below them (a solid line).

    Officers go from level 1 to level 10, with one skill point for every level after the first, so a level 1 ensign brings nothing to a battle. Ranks come with levels: Ensign at 1, Lieutenant at 2, Lt. Commander at 4, Commander at 6, Captain at 8. Nine points never fill a fifteen-skill pyramid, and the cheapest way to the apex is seven, so an officer at the top has still chosen.

    Every officer gains experience from each battle (more for a better result) and a little each turn aboard. Promote an officer once they have the experience for the next level; short of it, the rest can be paid in prestige. Transfer an officer out and the side gains their worth plus their banked experience in prestige, which hires an officer of a higher level from the pool.

    With Auto level on, points go up the centre to the apex as soon as they are earned (Auto 1 to 7 on the tree), then to the first open skill. Picking a skill by hand turns auto level off for that officer. Retrain takes every point back for a little prestige.

    Version 1: ranks were the levels, and each station had three separate branches of three skills.

    After the Starfleet Command 3 manual's officers (printed page 36: officers gain experience over a campaign, their worth and skills rising, and are swapped for more experienced ones for prestige; three skills to a station), in our own words and numbers.
