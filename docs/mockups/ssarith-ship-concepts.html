Three traditions, drafted for you

The Hunt's culture as written is one line ("The hunt is the only honest sport: the quarry must be worthy, the stalk patient, the kill earned and counted"), plus its field kit and "squat armoured hunting ships with broad prows, dark and mottled, built to wait unseen". A people builds in more than one way, so these are traditions inside the Hunt that disagree with each other. They are ours and provisional: name them, change them or replace them (survey H2, H3). Version 4: C is a field hull in place of nacelles; version 5: the hulls are faired, smooth from above and flat along the sides; version 6: the plating lies true on the angled faces; version 7: working detail (guns, bells, bay doors, thrusters and each tradition's own parts); version 8: plating modelled in Blender and rendered into textures, fine metal over it; version 3's C Course is withdrawn (owner: "No go for C").

  • A Old Lodge: ships handed down and refitted for generations. Plates of every age, riveted, bronze repairs, worn field camouflage; the top deck under a pitched roof; one original nacelle and one later refit; grapple emitters in the prow to hold the quarry close. From "worn gunmetal, camouflage", "the kill earned", "broad prows"; the lodge, the roof and the refits are ours.
  • B Trackers: the hunt won by waiting and watching. Tracking tubes along the spine; radiators folded down the flanks while it holds its heat in, swung out only when it hunts; drive vented sideways; dark mottled louvres. From "the stalk patient", "tracking tubes", "built to wait unseen", "dark and mottled".
  • C Field Hull (your direction): no nacelles. The field generators they carry sit in a secondary hull with rooms of its own (17 rooms on two decks, fitted the same way), joined by a neck, below and to one side, below, or above (Layout). The tradition is ours: yards that build the field drive as a hull apart, to shield it, change it or cut it loose after a long hunt. Smooth cast sections, dark above and pale below ("camouflage").

The stereotype test (Ship Design Bible C5)

Each described without naming the species:

    On the bible

    • Rooms first: 34 rooms on three decks, crew 40, the middle deck widest and each room aft a little narrower, so the hull tapers aft, and a room across the whole prow on the two lower decks, so the bow stays broad ("broad prows").
    • The hull is the shared fitter's, then character on top of it (rule H2): no steps where a deck ends, only slopes, and a broad prow; every room is measured 1.05 m inside.
    • Windows are the person-sized pane, one row a deck, on lived-in rooms only, lit dim phosphor green (the console's tubes).

    Version 1, withdrawn

    Owner, 2026-11-04: "you are over stereotyping an alien species which is extremely insensitive ... the look of an alien species is not their UI, not their spaceship". Version 1 took claws, scales and a reptile's sprawl from the species' body; all of it is gone.
