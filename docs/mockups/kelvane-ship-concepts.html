Notes

Version 1 (you: "begin designs on the institute ships"). Breadth first, as with the Ssarith Hunt: twelve massings at frigate size, so you can pick before anything is detailed.

The Institute's culture is still thin (one line: truth is measured, never assumed), so five traditions inside it are drafted for you to pick, change or reject. Each blockout names the one it comes from. Three are the traditions drafted for the emblem (the Calibrators, the Open Notebook, the Long Baseline); two are new (the Field Stations, the Collider Halls).

Each hull is one grown structure on the same code as the Hunt's ships (Ship Design Bible, chapter 14): one body, its arms, decks and spars split from its own section, and where two branches meet again the skin runs round the opening. No primitive is placed.

Version 3 (you: "l1 loop looks cool, can you make me some more variations of ships with loops? horizontal, vertical, both?"): seven more loop ships, 13 to 19, beside the Loop. Horizontal loops open seen from above, vertical ones from the side, and the last two both ways. Each is the hull parting round an opening and closing again, its inner walls lit as the beamline. Loop family shows them alone.

Version 4 (you: "12,11,18.19,17 all really good shapes, start detailing these, use more complex ones for larger captial ships like destroyer, cruiser, batlecruiser and carrier, we might want to theme them after advanced science and research vessels, this faction will specialize in scientific missions"): your five picks are the frigate, destroyer, cruiser, battlecruiser and carrier, 126 to 214 m, the more complex ones the larger ships. The frigate is 5 Slotted (you: "12 is no go use 5"). Each is grown again at its size with the structure its work needs: two identical instrument decks round a glazed slot, stepped instrument and laboratory decks with glass along their risers, a collision hall, a hangar deck and a launch well. The four larger ships come from the Collider Halls, the frigate from the Open Notebook. Class names are placeholders until you name the Institute's ships.

Version 5 (you: "start detailing these", then "Mind the clean and sterile and almost scientific aesthetics. They would have decorated their ships with smooth round pieces rather than industrial greebles"): every class is unwrapped on its own and baked from a modelled detail layer, as the Hunt's ships were. Calibrated seams in enamelled champagne, smooth round pods sunk into the plating, smoked-glass sensor domes, amber jewel lamps, red bands at the bow, the KID logo, a measuring scale engraved down each flank, and hull floods. No greebles, trenches or slatted vents. Painted and PBR show the bake; Champagne and Clay the grown skin; Night lights each ship by its own lamps. Windows, rooms, bays and damage come next.

The skin answers the console (chapter 11): rolled champagne edges like its plates, wells of dark smoked glass, amber light for a reading. Clay shows the massing alone.

None is drawn from the Kelvane's bodies (chapter 13). Described without the species' name, each is an instrument's or a laboratory's working shape, never a creature's.
