How a campaign is built

  • A pool, not a list. A campaign names about 50 missions it may offer. The repeatable ones are drawn again and again as the war goes on; the story ones once each.
  • Eras. Three maps, early, middle and late, so the frontier and the ships on offer change as the war does.
  • Story behind a gate. The story chain opens only once the captain's prestige passes a threshold (0 in a general campaign, 800 in a story one), so a captain earns the story with ordinary work first.
  • Chains. Each story mission names the one that follows, and some branch (a mission with two or three variants of its next step, chosen by the outcome).
  • One campaign per side and opponent. The same chain is written once and played from several sides: the briefing, the log and the debriefing come in a version for every side.

How a mission is built

Every mission is assembled from the same few pieces, which is what makes 150 of them possible.

Teams

Ships are grouped into teams by job: the player's team, the player's wingmen, allies, the enemy, neutral third parties, a planet, a base, freighters, transports, a monster, an attacking and a defending team (up to three of each in one mission), and props (cargo pods, buoys). A mission is mostly a choice of teams and where they start.

Actor states

Every team and ship runs a small state machine: an initial state at the start, a base state while the battle runs, and states for winning, losing or "an ally was attacked". Events (a ship destroyed, captured or disengaged; a scan done; a timer) move them on.

Victory conditions

A condition watches the battle and scores it: defend these, destroy those, scout that team, keep this team from scouting you. Conditions combine: a primary objective decides win or lose, a secondary lifts the grade, and "all objectives" is the best result.

Text with tokens

Lines carry blanks the game fills: the captain's name and rank, the player's empire and its plural, each team's empire, the system's name and the date. One line serves every captain.

What a story mission carries

  1. A briefing from the captain's superior, one version per side, ending in a dismissal.
  2. An opening log in the captain's own voice, in each side's form (a log, a journal, a war record).
  3. A one-line objective shown on the screen.
  4. Crew lines: a remark from each station (helm, comms, weapons, navigation, security, engineering, science) when the player asks them.
  5. Events in the battle: ships arriving "by unknown means", an ultimatum, a diplomat landing, a scan finishing, each with a line from the officer who would report it.
  6. Hails and choices: the player answers a hail from a short list (in the one read: surrender, or no reply), and allies answer the player.
  7. A debriefing per grade, and for some grades a different line depending on what was done (a ship captured, an order disobeyed for a good reason).
  8. The next mission.

Outcome grades

No mission is just won or lost. The reference game scores four levels, from a crushing defeat to an outstanding victory, plus a forfeit when the captain surrenders; its convoy and scouting debriefings show how the levels map onto a mission's own numbers (freighters through, ships scanned, the scout stopped or captured). Fleet Command's ladder adds a draw in the middle, for a part-done mission or an orderly withdrawal:

Crushing defeatNothing achieved; the career is questioned.
DefeatThe primary objective failed.
DrawPart done, or the captain withdrew in good order.
VictoryThe primary objective done.
OutstandingPrimary and secondary done, or a bonus (a capture, no losses).

Two lessons for Fleet Command: withdrawing is an outcome, not a failure (every generic mission orders the captain to disengage if outnumbered and grades it); and a grade can depend on judgement (a debriefing that forgives breaking an order when the captain faced an unknown, superior threat).

Battles outside the campaign

The skirmish and multiplayer scripts show the other ways a battle can be framed, worth keeping for the skirmish screen:

  • Free for all and team battle, with a timed variant (a limit, then scored).
  • Tournament rounds and a lighter version of each.
  • Random battle: sides and ships drawn for you.
  • Station assault as a team game, one side attacking, one defending.
  • A sport: ships push an object into a goal with tractor beams.
  • Wrecks: break up every class (our own test map already does this).

A story chain: the Krai's

The same events from the other side, so one chain serves both, as the reference game does, with its own lines:

  1. Blooding: a new warleader's first raid on an undefended relay (patrol).
  2. The Long Haul: escort the warhost's ore barges past a Concord picket (convoy escort).
  3. The Demand: the Krai side of Flag of truce; the player is the one who arrives and makes the demand, and chooses whether to fire.
  4. Hold the Line or Quiet Talks from the Krai side: break the orbit, or smuggle the saboteur out.
  5. The Second Fleet from the Krai side: keep the sleeping ships alive until they wake.
