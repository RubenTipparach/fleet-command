Analog Panel Bible

How to build interface panels the way 1990s skins and dashboards looked: a formed metal plate over a recessed, backlit display. Every part has a name and a code, so you can point at it and say what to change: "the N2 shoulder is too tight", "the F4 spill is too hot", "lower SPILL".

This guide goes with the skill .claude/skills/blender-ui-panels, which holds the Blender procedure. This page holds the vocabulary, the principles and the story of how we learnt them. The diagrams are drawn from the same outline functions the panels are built from (tools/mockups/ui_bible_svgs.py).

Chapter 1

The two layers

A panel is two things stacked and lit separately. On top is the metal: pillowed plates with windows cut through them, knobs and keys, rivets and bezels. Below it, in a well, is the display: a cream film that glows from behind, with its printing on it. The owner put it in one sentence:

Owner, 2026-10-30"this skin comprises of two layers, the top metal layer, and the back "display" layer that looks like it was indirectly lit from the top like a dashboard in an old car"

Cut through one window, screen-top on the left. The plate's top face is 8 mm up; the film sits 12 mm below it. Lamps (F3) hide 8 mm off the film just inside the window and throw their spill (F4) down the screen. The main light comes from the upper left, so the top lip throws the main shadow (W6). A sub-display (F6) is sunk a further 1.5 mm. Drawn at 7 px per mm.

The two layers never share light. The metal gets a soft room light, a softbox and a broad overhead light for polished parts to reflect. The display gets only its own backlight, its lamps and the main light that falls into the well. In Blender this is light linking: each light names the collection it may reach.

Chapter 2

Chapter 3

Depth

Depth is what makes a panel read as a made object and not a flat drawing. It comes from five places, and each one is a real piece of geometry or a real shadow, never a painted gradient.

  1. Rolled edges (P2). Every plate edge rolls over in a full round of 3.8 mm. Blender's curve bevel grows a shape by its radius (a 100 mm square with a 5 mm bevel comes out 110 mm wide), so the outside is drawn smaller by the roll and every opening larger, and the plate keeps its drawn footprint. A window 8 px in from the plate edge leaves one round bar between them: the frame bar (P3).
  2. The well (W2). The film sits 12 mm below the plate's top face, far enough down for the lamps to spread.
  3. The contact line (W5). Where the film meets the well it darkens in a thin line, from ambient occlusion (7 mm reach, power 2.2), so the film looks seated in the well.
  4. The main shadow (W6). The room's main light, from the upper left, also falls into the wells. The top lip casts a shadow onto the film's top edge and the left lip a thinner one along its left edge. This is what the reference had and we were missing: shadow on top and slightly on the left, hinting there is a main light.
  5. Displays within the display (F6). A readout box is a smaller film sunk 1.5 mm into the main one, with its own contact line and a small lamp along its top. Its printed frame (F7) sits on the step.

Gaps between plates show the dark chassis (P7), so they read as deep slots. Never let lit film show in a gap.

Chapter 4

Light

Three lights reach the film, each on its own setting so one can change without the others moving.

  • Backlight (F2). The film's own even glow, in cream #f4d9a9. Its strength, FILM_GLOW, sets the film's colour: at 1.0 the clear film measures #eacfa1 against the reference's #eacf9f. Keep it apart from the lamps: when the lamps were dimmed on one shared setting, the film went dark with them.
  • Lamps (F3) and spill (F4). Each lamp is a short line, 40 px long and 20 mm deep, tucked in the well just inside the top lip, 8 mm off the film. It points straight down the screen (90° from the film's normal) and spreads over 120°, so its light spills down the film as a soft fan. Three over a wide display, one over a narrow one. Power ×0.2 is the owner's chosen strength.
  • Main light (W6). The room light from the upper left, the same one that lights the metal, linked to the display too, so the lips throw their shadow.

What the lamps must never be: a strip along the whole top edge (it makes a highlight across the glass that reads as curved glass), aimed into the film (they make dots), or a point (a hot spot that washes out the text).

Chapter 5

Readability

A lit display must stay readable everywhere, right under a lamp included. Four rules do it.

  1. Ink absorbs light. Printing on the film (F5) reflects only 35% of its colour; its glow carries the full colour, faintly, through the film. Then a lamp right above it lights the film around the letters, not the letters.
  2. Ink is never black. It is the warm tan of printing on a backlit film, sampled from the reference: text and scales #9a7f4f, box outlines #7d6845, list text #b39867, a red trace #a83020.
  3. Print is flat. Everything on the film is flat, like the printing on a calculator's display: text with no depth, lines as flat ribbons, frames as flat rings, indicators as flat dots, none casting a shadow. Some fonts are built from overlapping outlines and fill with holes in Blender (Instrument Sans, Work Sans); use DejaVu Sans or Tektur on the film.
  4. Test it under the lamp. Print a line of text right under a lamp and tune the lamps until it reads. Measure the contrast as (film − ink) / film from the brightest and darkest pixels there. Keep it at 0.5 or more and the lamp's peak under 250 of 255.
Owner"To test Place some text directly under the light and tweak brightness/diffusal until you see the text"

The legibility test from the study: contrast directly under the middle lamp for each lamp setting tried. Drawn to scale.

Chapter 6

Analog feel

Analog means it looks manufactured: pressed, plated, printed and lit by bulbs. The choices that give that feel:

  • Pressed sheet metal. Buttons sit in soft dimples (C1) pressed into the plate, never in black sockets. Keys 22 × 16 px dimples 2.6 mm deep; knob seats 20 px at 1.2 mm.
  • Plated parts. A thin brass bezel (W4) under every lip; riveted sub-plates (P5) carrying button rows; engraved dividers.
  • Knobs that stand proud. A polished near-sphere (C3) on a dark collar (C2), its centre above the plate. Polished metal seen straight on reflects the room, so it needs a broad soft light overhead or it renders black.
  • Sliders as slots. A rolled slot (C6) through the plate with a thin gold streak (C7) glowing in its dark depth and a tick column (C8) either side. The track slider follows the same design as the equaliser's.
  • A sampled palette. Greige metal #cac1b0, cream film #eacf9f, brass #b0915a, dark chassis #2e271c, tan ink. Sample a reference's colours, don't guess them.
  • Bulbs, not LEDs. Warm lamp light with a soft edge, a little brighter at its source, fading as it runs down the film.
Chapter 7

Fit inside the parent

Every child element sits wholly inside its parent's inner edge, with an even margin: bars in their strip, keys on their plate, readouts in their well, knobs on their plate or notch. A child that hangs over its parent's edge cheapens the look.

Owner"make sure the UI fits well inside parent elements ... you can see these health sliders overlapping with its parent. not a hard rule, but generally we dont want that overlap, it cheapens the look"

The owner's example: on the console-skins page the Concord top strip's three bars run past the strip's bottom edge.

The exception is designed, not accidental. When metal must reach over a display, it is formed as part of its parent: a notch, a bridge, an ear (chapter 8). It is never a child spilling out of its box.

Chapter 8

Formed metal

The volume and balance notch is the shape the owner liked most, and the one a model gets wrong by default: it puts a rounded rectangle on top of the plate. A real panel is one piece of metal, pressed with its display window, so the window itself has a custom, non-square shape and the metal flows up into it.

Owner"You cant just slap a primitive on , you have to merge that shape with the panel. I want more of these elaborate single pieces of metal to create uis with. It lets you place buttons over displays without having to rely solely on the 2d display part."

How the notch works

The window's outline is cut with a smooth rise. Along the bottom edge, the metal lifts by h within hw of the centre, and falls back to the edge over fall px through a smoothstep. One smoothstep gives both curves: a concave foot fillet (N3) where it leaves the edge and a convex shoulder (N2) at the top. Then the plate is built from that outline, so its rolled edge (P2) follows the notch; the bezel (W4) and the film follow the same outline. The knobs sit in shallow dimples on the flat (N4).

The study's notch: rise 30 px, flat 148 px, fall 30 px. Keep the fall at least as wide as the rise, or it reads as a cut instead of a pressing; keep a knob inside the flat with 6 px of metal round it.

The catalogue

Every shape below is built the same way: describe the window's edges as profiles, build the plate, bezel and film from that one outline, then cut dimples and seats. Each diagram is drawn by the outline function named under it. The notch is built and rendered; the others are designs with their recipes, ready to build.

Chapter 11

Worked example

Everything above was learnt in one session, refining a Blender study after the Nucleo NLog Winamp skin, by its own author. The study is a learning exercise, not shipped game art: it follows someone else's layout, so it is not part of the game. The images are our own renders, with our own text. Here is how it went, step by step, and what each step taught.

1 · Two layers

Owner"the top metal layer, and the back "display" layer ... indirectly lit from the top like a dashboard in an old car"

The first build: a satin plate with windows and a cream film behind. The geometry was there, but one room light lit everything evenly, so nothing glowed.

Lesson: the display needs its own light. Chapter 1.

2 · Backlit, too bright

Owner"oooh you gotta back lit it" · "too bright, avoid lighting the metal part with backlight"

The film became emissive with uneven bulb hot spots. It washed the whole panel out, metal included.

Lesson: keep the backlight flat and inside the windows.

3 · Contained

Owner"back light should be a small flat light cont"

Light linking split the layers: room lights reach only the metal, the film's light only the display.

Lesson: light linking is the foundation. Chapter 1.

4 · Contact shadow

Owner"note the contact shadows around the rim, to make it look like theres some inset" · "the light kind of illuminates just the top o that shadow"

Ambient occlusion darkened the film where the plate overhangs it.

Lesson: W5, the contact line. Chapter 3.

5 · Lamps, not a strip

Owner"notice how the original was 3 spot light, 3 spotlight and 1 spot light"

The strip lamps became discrete spots: three over each wide display, one over the narrow one. They came out as small dots.

Lesson: count and place the lamps; aim decides their shape. Chapter 4.

6 · Wider, softer

Owner"wider and more diffused lighting. the frame on the ui is a little thick"

Bigger, softer sources, and the frame round each window went from 17 px to 11 px.

Lesson: a soft source needs room. Chapter 4.

7 · A deeper well

With the lamps 3 mm off the film the light piled up beside them. The well went from 6 to 12 mm, so the lamps sit about 10 mm up and spread.

Lesson: W2 sets how far light can travel. Chapter 3.

8 · The reference's palette

Owner"the middle panel is missing a light, the light is a bit bright, you also need to follow this color pallete"

Colours sampled from the reference: cream film, greige metal, tan ink. A lamp went into the middle panel. The polished dials rendered black: seen straight on, they mirrored the dark room.

Lesson: sample, don't guess; polished parts need something bright to reflect. Chapter 6.

9 · Hard-surface dials

Owner"the dials have more grooves" · "some hard surface modeling with booleans is required in blender to get the buttons, grroves and bevel details right"

Dials built from primitives: a dish cut by a sphere, grooves cut by torus rings, knurling cut by boxes, bevelled after.

Lesson: detail is cut, then bevelled. The skill's "cut, bake, dissolve, bevel".

10 · Pressed and plated

Owner"the bevel and 3d details on the metal plates just doesnt quite have the same aesthetic"

The metal became pressed sheet: dimples cut by ellipsoids, brass bezels, a riveted transport plate with engraved dividers, dark gaps between plates. A boolean left a sliver; baking the cuts and dissolving flat areas before bevelling fixed it.

Lesson: analog is manufactured. Chapter 6.

11 · Compare the pixels

Owner"you wanna compare the pixels? and see what we did wrong?"

Measured side by side at the same size: our film was murky tan #ac9874 against #eacf9f; our metal's contrast was 26 against 54; the reference's plate edges read 120, 66, 254, 199, 81, 254, 51, two crisp highlights and two dark lines. After the fixes: film #e9ce9f, contrast 61.

Lesson: measure before changing; the skill lists the method.

12 · Rounder metal

Owner"Yea their metal is more rounded looking."

We found that Blender's curve bevel was growing every plate by its radius. Drawing the outline smaller first let each plate roll over in a full 3.8 mm round, and the frame round each window became one round bar.

Lesson: P2 and P3, footprint-true. Chapter 3.

13 · The notch

Owner"The top dash needs that bump, this things that looks like a controls continuous piece of metal rather than your rounded rectangle" · "The notch design is great" · "The top two panels are actually one panel with a big slider for the music track progress slider."

The separate tab became a notch cut into the window's own outline, and two plates became one with a progress slot.

Lesson: formed metal. Chapter 8.

14 · Text and proud knobs

Owner"add text, also the nobs arent right, they shoud pop out like 3d knobs" · "sample text color and line colors from the original, the back plate stuff was not a solid black ... it had sort of fainter quality"

Our own labels and track names; knobs became near-spheres on collars; ink became sampled tans that glow faintly through the film. Next and previous went smaller than play, pause and stop; the track slider took the equaliser's design.

Lesson: ink is never black. Chapter 5.

15 · Aim the lamps

Owner"spot pools are just dots, it should look like spot light spilling on the sturface" · "bottoms is best" · "make it a short line" · "Narrow it back to 120 per cone"

A sweep of the lamp's tilt: 30° and 35° made dots, 60° started a spill, 80° and 90° fanned down the film. The owner picked 90°; the lamp became a short line with a 120° spread. The front glass was made flat by removing the strip lamps.

Lesson: LIP_TILT, LIP_CONE. Chapter 9.

16 · Read it under the lamp

Owner"Brightness on the light is still too strong make it more diffused. So that I can actually read the text" · "treat the text and ui elements like a calculator"

Text printed right under the lamp. At every power, the letters under the lamp turned bright yellow: the ink reflected the lamp as well as the film did.

Lesson: measure legibility where it is worst. Chapter 5.

17 · Ink that absorbs

Owner"Power x0.2 is the sweet spot"

The ink's surface now reflects 35% of its colour. Contrast under the lamp went from 0.14 at the start to 0.62, with the lamp no longer clipping. All display elements went flat with no shadows.

Lesson: ink absorbs. Chapter 5.

18 · Depth and colour

Owner"Also another thing you're missing is a display with in a display" · "somewhere along the way we changed the color of the display" · "notice theres more shadow on top and slightly on left side of displays, htiing that there is a main light"

Readout boxes became sub-displays sunk 1.5 mm. The film's glow and the lamps got separate settings, which brought back the matched colour. The main light now reaches the displays, so the lips cast the top and left shadow.

Lesson: F6, FILM_GLOW and LAMPS apart, W6. Chapters 3 and 4.

Chapter 12

Before you call it done

  • Every child inside its parent with an even margin; every reach-over is formed metal (chapters 7, 8).
  • The film's clear colour matches the target; gaps show the dark chassis.
  • Every lamp spills down the film; no strip highlight; no clipped hot spot (peak under 250).
  • Text right under a lamp reads at contrast 0.5 or more; nothing on the film casts a shadow.
  • Polished knobs read as metal, not black; dimples and bezels catch the light.
  • Wells show their contact line and the main shadow on top and left.
  • Look at a 2x crop of every changed area, and compare against the reference by the numbers.
